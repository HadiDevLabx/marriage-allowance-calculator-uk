# Marriage Allowance Calculator UK — Is It Worth £252 to You?

> Transferring £1,260 of personal allowance saves a couple up to £252 a year, and you can backdate four years.

Reference notes for the UK tax year **2026/27**, computed from HMRC's
published rates rather than copied from another site.

## How it works

One partner transfers £1,260 of unused personal allowance to the other. It works only if:

- The lower earner has income **below** £12,570
- The higher earner is a **basic-rate** taxpayer, so under £50,270

£1,260 at 20% is £252, which is the maximum.

The partial cases matter and most calculators skip them. If the lower earner has income between £11,310 and £12,570, transferring still helps but the lower earner starts paying some tax, so the household gain is less than £252.

Claims can be backdated four tax years, which is worth up to about £1,000 in one go.

## What this does not cover

If the higher earner is a higher-rate taxpayer you are not eligible at all. Marriage Allowance is different from the Married Couple's Allowance, which applies only where one partner was born before 6 April 1935.

## Source and tax year

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs 6 April 2026
to 5 April 2027, and were checked against
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) on 28 August 2026.

Rates change every April, and often at a Budget. Check the tax year a figure
belongs to before relying on it.
